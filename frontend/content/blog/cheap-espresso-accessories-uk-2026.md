Espresso is the one drink where the gear between the beans and the cup genuinely changes the result. That makes it fertile ground for spending, and the ceiling is high: an [Acaia Lunar, the scale most espresso bars use, is £320](https://www.maudecoffee.co.uk/products/acaia-lunar-espresso-coffee-scale), with UK retailers ranging from about £315 to £335.

The floor is much lower than people assume. Everything below is under forty pounds, all from MHW-3BOMBER, and each piece fixes one specific, identifiable fault rather than promising a vague improvement.

But there is an order to this, and getting it wrong is how people end up with a drawer of tools and the same bad coffee.

## Start here: none of this fixes your grinder

If your espresso is inconsistent, the grinder is the most likely cause and by some distance. Grind quality determines how evenly water can pass through the puck, and no accessory compensates for a burr set that cannot produce a consistent particle size.

So the honest sequence is: sort the grinder first, then buy the tools below. Bought in that order they are excellent value. Bought instead of a decent grinder, they are a drawer of metal that changes nothing you can taste.

That is the whole caveat. Everything from here assumes the grind is already reasonable and you want to stop wasting it.

## Frequently asked

**Which one should I buy first?** The WDT tool, assuming your grinder is decent. Channelling is the fault that ruins the most shots, and stirring out clumps is the cheapest way to stop it.

**Do I need the distributor and the WDT tool?** Not really. They both address puck preparation from different angles, and most people settle on one. If you are choosing, the WDT tool addresses the deeper problem.

**Will 58mm fit my machine?** Check before ordering. 58mm is the common size on prosumer machines, while Sage and Breville home machines are usually 54mm, which is why the dosing funnel above is the 54mm version. Getting this wrong is the most common mistake in this category.

**Is a £320 scale really nine times better?** No, and nobody sensible claims it is. It is faster to respond, measures flow rate live, and is built for a bar counter being used a hundred times a day. For one or two coffees each morning, 0.1g and a timer is the part that matters.

**Will any of this make cheap beans taste good?** No. Fresh beans and a decent grinder come first, then these, then the machine. Spending in the wrong order is why people conclude that home espresso is not worth it.
