## Why people look for a Fitbit alternative

Usually it comes down to one of three things. The price still feels steep even on sale. Some people prefer a different app, like Samsung Health or Google Fit, over Fitbit's own. Others just want a second tracker for the household without paying full Fitbit price twice. All three of these come down to price and trust in the brand, not a random cheap band with no real name behind it.

## Frequently asked

**Is the Galaxy Fit3 as accurate as a Fitbit?** Samsung's sensors are well regarded for heart-rate and sleep tracking, but Fitbit has more history building coaching features on top of that raw data. For basic step, heart-rate, and sleep tracking, both do the job well.

**Does either work with an iPhone?** The Charge 6 does, fully, through Fitbit's own app. The Fit3 connects but loses some features on iPhone, so we wouldn't recommend it as an iPhone-first purchase.

**Is a fitness band the same thing as a smartwatch?** Not quite. Both trackers here are bands built for fitness tracking with basic notifications, not full smartwatches that run apps, like a Galaxy Watch or Apple Watch. If you want app support and calls from your wrist, that's a different product category entirely.

**How long do fitness bands like these usually last before needing a replacement?** With normal daily wear and charging, most bands in this price range hold up well for two to three years before battery life noticeably declines. That's worth factoring into the price comparison, since a cheaper band that needs replacing sooner isn't automatically the better deal.
