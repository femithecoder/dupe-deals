Search "Fitbit Charge 6 alternative" and most results compare it to the £139.99 RRP. That makes almost anything look cheap. But the Charge 6 [regularly sells for £79 to £90](https://www.trustedreviews.com/deals/googles-fitbit-charge-6-is-now-below-90-saving-you-50-for-prime-day) at the best current UK deals, sometimes even under £100 without a special code. A fair alternative needs to beat that real price, not the one on the box.

## Why people look for a Fitbit alternative

Usually it comes down to one of three things: the price still feels steep, even on sale; a preference for a different app (Samsung Health or Google Fit instead of Fitbit's own); or just wanting a second tracker without paying full Fitbit price twice. All three come down to price and trust in the brand, not a random cheap band with no name behind it.

## The one that actually beats Fitbit's price

### [Samsung Galaxy Fit3](/product/33): £76.94 (was £85.99)

![Samsung Galaxy Fit3 fitness tracker](https://assets.media-quzo.co.uk/site/catalogue/large/2024/11/29/samsung-galaxy-fit3-4-06-cm-1-6-amoled-touchscreen-0-256-gb-18-5-g-sm-r390nzsaeub-391973.webp)

A 1.6-inch AMOLED touchscreen band with heart-rate and sleep tracking, made by a name you'd recognise. At £76.94, it beats even Fitbit's best real sale price, not just the RRP most "alternative" articles use. We checked that against Fitbit's actual current UK deals, not the number on the box.

**Battery life:** Up to 13 days with the always-on screen turned off, or about 3.5 days with it left on. That's genuinely good, though not miles ahead of the Charge 6's own battery life.

**Water resistance:** Rated 5ATM and IP68 — built for swimming, rain, and washing your hands, and tested to survive 30 minutes at 1.5 metres deep. Not built for diving or a pressure washer.

**iPhone compatibility, honestly:** Most "alternative" roundups skip this. The Fit3 is made for Android and works best with Samsung phones. It will connect to an iPhone (iOS 12+), but Samsung says plainly that you won't get every feature that way. If you're on iPhone and want everything working properly, that's the one real reason to stick with Fitbit's own app.

What you lose versus the Charge 6, if you're on Android: Fitbit's coaching features and its wider app ecosystem, if you're already using it. If you're not already a Fitbit user, that's barely a loss at all.

**Pros:**
- Beats Fitbit's real sale price, not just its RRP
- Long battery life (up to 13 days)
- Proper water resistance for swimming and rain

**Cons:**
- Reduced features on iPhone
- Fitbit still wins on coaching tools and app ecosystem

**Verdict:** A rare "alternative" that actually beats the real price, as long as you're on Android or don't mind losing a few features on iPhone.

## Frequently asked

**Is the Galaxy Fit3 as accurate as a Fitbit?** Samsung's sensors are well-regarded for heart-rate and sleep tracking, but Fitbit has more history with coaching features built on top of that data. For basic tracking, both do the job well.

**Does either work with an iPhone?** The Charge 6 does, fully, through Fitbit's own app. The Fit3 connects but loses some features on iPhone, so we wouldn't recommend it as an iPhone-first buy.

**Is a fitness band the same as a smartwatch?** Not quite. Both trackers here are bands built for fitness tracking with basic notifications — not full smartwatches with apps, like a Galaxy Watch or Apple Watch.
