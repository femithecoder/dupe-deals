Screen-free audio players solve a specific problem: a child who wants a story at bedtime, and a parent who does not want to hand over a tablet to provide one.

The two names everyone knows are Toniebox and Yoto. A Toniebox 2 is around £99.95 and a Yoto Player around £89.99. Those are reasonable prices, and they are also not the number that decides what this costs you.

**The device is the small purchase. The content is the large one.** That is the thing worth understanding before buying anything in this category, and it is where the alilo player below takes a genuinely different approach.

## Where the money actually goes

Toniebox and Yoto both work on physical content you buy: a Tonie figure for the former, a card for the latter. Tonies are roughly £14.99 each. Yoto cards start around £2.99 and climb.

That model has real advantages. A child choosing a figure, putting it on the box and hearing it start is a satisfying ritual, and the physical object is half the appeal. Collections grow, get given as presents, and last.

It also means the running total keeps moving. A Toniebox with five figures is around £175 rather than £100. Whether that is a problem depends entirely on how you feel about buying content, and plenty of families are glad to.

The alternative is a player that arrives with the content already on it. No cards, no figures, no ongoing spend, and no collecting ritual either. That is the trade, stated plainly, and neither answer is wrong.

## Frequently asked

**Is preloaded content a disadvantage?** It would be on a closed device, because you would be stuck with what shipped. Bluetooth removes most of that, since anything you can play on a phone you can send to the speaker. What it does not replicate is a child independently choosing something without asking an adult, which is the real appeal of cards and figures.

**Which is best for a toddler?** The physical systems, generally. Putting an object on a box is a simpler action than navigating buttons, and independence is much of the point at that age.

**Do these need Wi-Fi?** The card-based players need setup and then largely work offline. The alilo works out of the box. If your concern is a device that phones home, all of these are far less connected than a tablet, which is usually why people are shopping in this category.

**Is a white noise machine actually necessary?** No. It is useful if you are already using a phone for the job or if the room is noisy. If nobody is having trouble sleeping, it is a solution looking for a problem.

**What about just using a smart speaker?** It works, and it is cheaper if you own one. What you lose is a device a child can operate alone without a voice assistant listening, which for some households is the entire reason for buying a separate box.
