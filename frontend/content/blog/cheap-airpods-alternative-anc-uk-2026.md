Apple's AirPods 4 list at £119 on [Apple's own UK store](https://www.apple.com/uk/shop/buy-airpods/airpods-4), and [sell for less again on a good deal](https://www.techradar.com/news/cheapest-airpods-prices). That lower figure, not the list price, is the real number to beat. It's also worth knowing upfront that the base AirPods 4 don't come with active noise cancellation at all. You'd need the pricier ANC version, £169 from Apple, for that.

## What "cheap AirPods alternative" usually gets wrong

Most searches for this land on generic, no-name earbuds from marketplace listings. There's no real brand behind them and the specs are hard to check. That's a real risk with wireless earbuds specifically, since fit, seal, and how well the noise cancelling actually works are hard to judge from a listing alone. A name you can actually look up matters more here than in most product categories.

## Our pick of five

We list five sets below, all with real active noise cancelling, all cheaper than the ANC version of AirPods 4. They are not equally good, and we have said which is which rather than pretending every option is a winner.

## Frequently asked

**Do these work properly with an iPhone?** Yes, over standard Bluetooth and AAC. The LDAC Hi-Res codec specifically is an Android feature. iPhone users still get full ANC, calls, and touch controls.

**Is 45dB ANC actually noticeable, or just a number on a spec sheet?** Real ANC at this level noticeably cuts steady background noise, like traffic, air conditioning, or office chatter, though no ANC earbuds fully block sudden sharp sounds like a door slamming. It's a genuine, working feature here, not marketing.

**Is IP54 good enough for the gym?** Yes, for sweat and light rain. It isn't rated for swimming. If you need that, you're looking at a different kind of earbud entirely, usually with a higher IP rating on both the buds and the case.

**Is it worth paying more than the cheapest option?** Usually, yes, though not always. The gap between the cheapest set here and the mid-priced ones is mostly certainty: at the bottom you are trusting a specification nobody has independently checked. Pay a bit more and you are buying earbuds several reviewers have actually used and written about, which for something that lives in your ears every day is worth the difference.

**How does the touch control system work in daily use?** Each earbud has a pressure-sensitive panel on the stem, used to play or pause music, skip tracks, answer calls, and switch listening modes. It takes a short adjustment period if you're used to a physical button, but most people get comfortable with it within a day or two.
