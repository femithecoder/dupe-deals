Lightweight strollers are sold on a phrase, and the phrase does not mean what people think.

"Cabin approved" is not a certification. There is no single standard behind it, no body that awards it, and no guarantee that a stroller carrying the label will be accepted by the airline you are flying with. Airlines set their own hand luggage rules, those rules differ, and they change.

Most carriers that allow a folded stroller in an overhead locker are working to something around **55 x 40 x 20cm**, and generally want the whole thing under about **7kg**. Those are the numbers that decide it, and they are the numbers you should be looking for rather than the phrase.

## The four figures that matter

**Folded dimensions.** All three of them, measured with the wheels and handle as they sit when folded, not the marketing silhouette. This is the one that gets people turned away at the gate.

**Total weight.** You will be lifting it above your head into a locker while holding a child. Under 7kg is the usual working figure, and the difference between 6kg and 9kg is enormous when your other arm is full.

**Fold mechanism.** One-handed matters for the same reason. A two-handed fold assumes somewhere to put the baby down, which at an airport gate you do not have.

**Recline and seat length.** A stroller that does not lie flat enough is not suitable for a young baby, and one with a short seat is outgrown quickly. Travel strollers trade both away for compactness, and how much they trade varies a lot.

Check all four against your specific airline's published hand luggage page, a week or two before you fly rather than the night before.

## Frequently asked

**Is "cabin approved" meaningless then?** Not meaningless, but it is a claim by the seller rather than an approval by anyone. It usually means the maker believes it fits common overhead locker dimensions. Your airline is the only authority on whether it does.

**Can I take a pushchair to the gate regardless?** Most airlines let you take a pushchair to the aircraft door and reclaim it there, which is a different thing from taking it into the cabin. If gate-checking is acceptable to you, the folded dimensions matter far less and you can ignore most of this page.

**Are cheap strollers less safe?** Pushchairs sold in the UK must meet BS EN 1888, which covers brakes, stability and restraints, and that applies at every price. What you tend to give up lower down the market is fabric quality, wheel smoothness and how long the fold mechanism keeps working, rather than the safety basics.

**Is a travel stroller enough as an only pushchair?** For some families, yes. The compromises are recline, seat length and how they handle rough pavements, so if it is your only one, weight those against the convenience.

**What about newborns?** Check the recline and whether the maker states a minimum age or a lie-flat position. Many lightweight strollers are not suitable from birth without a separate newborn attachment.
