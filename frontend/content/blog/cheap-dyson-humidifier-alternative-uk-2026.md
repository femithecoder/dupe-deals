Dry indoor air is a winter problem with a cheap fix and an expensive one. The expensive one is a [Dyson Humidify+Cool, £699 at John Lewis](https://www.johnlewis.com/dyson-humidify-cool-ph2de-nox-air-purifier-white-white/p114275497). The cheap one starts around thirty pounds.

That is a gap of roughly twenty times, which is large enough that the sensible reaction is suspicion rather than excitement. So the question worth answering is not whether a cheap humidifier is cheaper. It is what the other six hundred and sixty pounds is actually buying.

## The one thing a cheap humidifier cannot do

A Dyson Humidify+Cool is not a humidifier. It is three machines sharing one body: an air purifier, a humidifier, and a fan.

The purifier is the part that matters here. Dyson's own listing describes three-stage filtration, HEPA H13 with a carbon layer and catalytic oxidisation, aimed at dust, odours and allergens. None of the units below do any of that. They add moisture to the air and nothing else. If you have hay fever or a dust allergy, or you live on a main road and want something done about the air rather than the dryness, a cheap humidifier does not replace a purifier. We would rather say that at the top than bury it.

If, on the other hand, your problem is a dry throat every morning through winter, static, or a nursery that gets uncomfortable when the heating is on, then humidifying is the whole job, and that job costs about thirty pounds.

The fan is the third function and the easiest to replace. A [separate fan](/blog/cheap-dyson-fan-alternative-uk-2026) costs a fraction of the difference.

## Frequently asked

**Will a cheap humidifier clean my air?** No. Not one of these filters anything. They add water vapour. A machine that purifies as well is a different product and it costs several times more, which is exactly the point of the table above.

**Is a Dyson humidifier a rip-off then?** No. Calling it one would be easy and wrong. It genuinely does three jobs, and doing them in one machine on one plug in one bit of floor space has real value. What we would say is that plenty of people buy one for a problem that only needs the cheapest of the three functions.

**What about running costs?** All of them need cleaning and descaling, and that is not optional: a neglected humidifier grows things you then breathe. Dyson's own pitch leans heavily on hygiene for this reason. On a cheap unit you do the work yourself instead.

**How big a tank do I need?** Bigger tanks mean fewer refills, and refill fatigue is the reason most humidifiers end up in a cupboard by spring. If it is going in a bedroom you use every night, size up.

**Do I need warm mist?** Not usually. It is more comfortable in a cold room and uses more power. It is a preference, not a requirement.

## The bottom line

If you want purified air, buy the purifier. Nothing in this list is a substitute for one, and a cheap humidifier bought for that reason is money wasted.

If you want moisture in a dry room, the [Levoit 25dB](/product/45) does that job for the price of a takeaway, and the [4.2L version](/product/74) does it for longer between refills. The rest of what a Dyson does is real, and you should only pay for it if you actually want it.
