Most articles on this subject open by comparing a twenty pound fan to a seven hundred pound Dyson. That comparison is rigged, so this one will not make it.

Dyson's cheapest fan at [John Lewis](https://www.johnlewis.com/browse/electricals/heaters-fans-dehumidifiers/fans/dyson/_/N-ahbZ1z140en) is the HushJet Mini at £99. Our quietest tower fan is {{price:78}}. Those are close enough that anyone claiming a twenty-times saving is comparing a desk fan to a machine that also purifies the air and heats the room.

So here is the narrower, more useful question: where is the gap actually large, and where is Dyson not really charging much of a premium at all?

## Where the gap is real, and where it is not

Two things drive Dyson's fan prices, and only one of them is about moving air.

The first is the bladeless design, which is genuinely good engineering: nothing to trap fingers, nothing awkward to clean, and a smoother airflow than a caged blade. You pay for it. A [Dyson Cool CF1 desk fan is £249](https://www.johnlewis.com/browse/electricals/heaters-fans-dehumidifiers/fans/dyson/_/N-ahbZ1z140en). A desk fan that does the same job of pointing moving air at you is {{price:76}}. That gap is real and it is enormous.

The second is that most of Dyson's tower-shaped machines are not fans. They are purifiers or heaters that also blow air, priced from £399 to £699. Comparing those to a fan is comparing a different product, which is the trick this article is trying not to play. If you want purified air, [that is a separate conversation](/blog/cheap-dyson-humidifier-alternative-uk-2026) and a cheap fan is not part of it.

What Dyson does not currently sell at John Lewis is a plain, fan-only tower at a mid price. That is the gap the units below sit in.

## Frequently asked

**Is a bladeless fan actually better?** For safety and cleaning, yes, clearly. For cooling, it is a different feel rather than a straightforwardly better one. Nobody should spend two hundred pounds expecting to feel twice as cool.

**Why is the Dyson desk fan £249 when their smallest is £99?** Because they are different sizes doing different jobs, which is the same reason our range runs from {{price:76}} to {{price:79}}. Size and airflow drive fan prices far more than brand does.

**Does a cheap fan cool a room?** No fan cools a room. Fans move air across skin, which makes you feel cooler while the room temperature stays put or rises slightly. This is true of a {{price:76}} fan and a £699 one alike, and it is worth knowing before spending either amount.

**What about noise?** This is where cheap fans genuinely can be bad, and it is why both Levoit units above are picked on their 20dB rating rather than their airflow. Check the decibel figure before the price.

**Should I just buy the Dyson?** If you want the bladeless design and the build, buy it. At £99 for the HushJet the premium is small enough that talking you out of it would be silly.

That last distinction does more work here than any measurement would.
