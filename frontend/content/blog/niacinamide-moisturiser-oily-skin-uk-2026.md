Niacinamide, also known as vitamin B3, has become a go-to ingredient for oily and blemish-prone skin. There's a real reason for that. It helps control oil and makes pores look smaller, without the irritation that stronger ingredients like retinol or acids can cause. Most niacinamide content focuses on serums. Wanting it in your everyday moisturiser, rather than as a separate step, narrows the choices sharply. Wanting it at a fair price narrows them further.

## What to actually look for

Not every "oil control" moisturiser does the same job. The good ones pair niacinamide with something that actually helps at the source, like saw palmetto extract, a natural oil-regulator, or zinc. Skip the ones that just rely on powder or silica to mattify the surface. Those wear off by lunchtime and don't address why your skin is producing extra oil in the first place. A quick check before buying: read the full ingredient list, not the front of the bottle. Niacinamide should appear near the top, not buried at the end.

## Frequently asked

**Can you use niacinamide every day?** Yes. It is one of the gentler actives. There are none of the sun-sensitivity issues that come with retinol or acids, so it suits morning and evening use.

**Will this stop oily skin from being oily?** It should noticeably cut down on excess oil with regular use, but oily skin is mostly genetic and hormonal, so no moisturiser removes it completely. The realistic result is less shine and smaller-looking pores, not a different skin type altogether.

**Is this suitable for acne, not just oily skin?** Niacinamide and saw palmetto are commonly used for hormonal breakouts, but this isn't a medical treatment. If you have ongoing or severe acne, talk to a dermatologist alongside any changes to your skincare routine.

**How long before you actually see results?** Most people notice less midday shine within two to three weeks of daily use. Changes to pore appearance tend to take longer, often six to eight weeks, since that depends on longer-term oil regulation rather than an immediate effect.
