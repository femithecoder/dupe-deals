Niacinamide (vitamin B3) has become a go-to ingredient for oily and blemish-prone skin, and for good reason: it helps control oil and makes pores look smaller, without the irritation that stronger ingredients like retinol or acids can cause. Most niacinamide content focuses on serums. If you want it in your everyday moisturiser instead of a separate step, the choices get a lot smaller — especially at a genuinely fair price.

## What to actually look for

Not every "oil control" moisturiser does the same job. The good ones pair niacinamide with something that actually helps at the source — like saw palmetto extract (a natural oil-regulator) or zinc — not just powder or silica that mattifies the surface and wears off by lunchtime.

Use it as your last hydrating step before SPF in the morning, or your final step at night. ## Frequently asked

**Can you use niacinamide every day?** Yes — it's one of the gentler actives, without the sun-sensitivity issues that come with retinol or acids, so it works for both morning and evening.

**Will this stop oily skin from being oily?** It should noticeably cut down on excess oil with regular use, but oily skin is mostly genetic and hormonal — no moisturiser removes it completely. The realistic result is less shine and smaller-looking pores, not a different skin type.

**Is this suitable for acne, not just oily skin?** Niacinamide and saw palmetto are commonly used for hormonal breakouts, but this isn't a medical treatment. If you have ongoing or severe acne, talk to a dermatologist alongside any skincare changes.
