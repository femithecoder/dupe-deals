Niacinamide (vitamin B3) has become the go-to ingredient for oily and blemish-prone skin because it genuinely does what the marketing claims: it regulates sebum production and helps refine the look of enlarged pores, without the irritation risk that comes with stronger actives like retinol or high-strength acids. Most niacinamide content online focuses on serums. If you want it in your actual daily moisturiser instead of as a separate step, the options narrow considerably, especially at a genuinely affordable price.

## What to actually look for

Not every "oil control" moisturiser is built the same way. The ones worth considering combine niacinamide with something to genuinely regulate oil at the source, not just mattify on the surface with silica or powder fillers that wear off by midday. Ingredients like saw palmetto extract (a natural DHT-blocker often used for hormonal oil production) and zinc are the ones actually doing work here, not just the niacinamide headline ingredient alone.

## Where it fits in a routine

Niacinamide moisturisers work best as the last hydrating step before SPF in the morning, or the final step at night, sealing in whatever serums or treatments went underneath rather than fighting them. Niacinamide itself is unusually well-tolerated alongside other actives, including vitamin C and most acids, so layering concerns are lower here than with something like retinol, though as always, patch test a new product before working it into a full routine, particularly if you're combining it with other active treatments for the first time.

## Frequently asked

**Can you use niacinamide every day?** Yes, it's one of the better-tolerated actives specifically because it doesn't typically cause the sensitivity or sun-sensitivity issues associated with retinol or acids, making it suitable for both morning and evening use.

**Will a niacinamide moisturiser stop oily skin from being oily?** It should meaningfully reduce excess sebum production over consistent use, but oily skin is largely genetic and hormonal, no moisturiser eliminates it entirely. The realistic goal is less midday shine and smaller-looking pores, not a permanent skin-type change.

**Is this suitable for acne-prone skin, not just oily skin?** The saw palmetto and niacinamide combination is commonly used for hormonal breakout patterns specifically, but this isn't marketed or formulated as a medical acne treatment, if you have persistent or severe acne, that's worth a dermatologist conversation alongside any skincare routine change.
