For years a robot vacuum meant spending [iRobot Roomba](https://www.irobot.co.uk/) money to get one that actually navigated your home properly. That's no longer true. The gap between a budget robot and a flagship has narrowed to a few specific things: raw suction, mapping smarts, and self-emptying docks. The honest question isn't "what's the cheapest robot vacuum," it's how much suction and intelligence you actually need for your floors, and what you're really giving up by paying less. We list three at very different prices, so this is a straight comparison up the range.

## Why people look for a budget robot vacuum

Usually value plus realism. Most homes are mostly hard floors and light rugs, where you don't need flagship 10,000-plus-Pa suction to get a clean daily pass. Some people want a robot purely for maintenance cleaning between proper vacuums, not to replace them. And plenty just don't want to spend Roomba or Dyson money on a first robot they're not sure they'll love. None of those need a premium unit. They need a robot that navigates sensibly and picks up everyday dust and crumbs.

## What you'd actually be giving up versus a Roomba

Being straight about it: premium iRobot Roomba and top roborock models add self-emptying docks (weeks of hands-off cleaning), the most advanced obstacle avoidance, and the most reliable multi-floor mapping. Those are genuinely nice, especially the self-emptying dock. What they aren't is necessary to get clean floors. For most homes, the suction and navigation on a mid-priced robot like the Q7 M5 clean just as well day to day; you're mostly paying the flagship premium for convenience features, not cleaning power.

## Frequently asked

**Is a cheap robot vacuum worth it?** For hard floors and maintenance cleaning, yes, a budget robot like the Lefant keeps dust and crumbs down with automated daily passes. Where cheap robots fall short is deep-carpet suction and smart mapping, which is exactly what the step-up models add.

**Lefant or roborock, which should I buy?** If your home is mostly hard floors and you want the lowest price, the Lefant. If you have carpet or pets, the roborock Q7 M5's 10,000Pa suction and mopping make it the better main robot for not much more.

**Do I need 10,000Pa suction?** Only really for carpet and pet hair. On hard floors, the Lefant's 2,200Pa is enough for everyday debris. Suction matters most when you're pulling dirt up out of fibres rather than off a smooth surface.

**What does a Roomba do that these don't?** Mainly self-emptying docks and the most advanced obstacle avoidance and mapping. Those add convenience, not fundamentally cleaner floors, which is why a mid-priced robot cleans comparably day to day.

**Can a robot vacuum replace my normal vacuum?** For daily upkeep on hard floors, largely yes. For deep-cleaning thick carpet and stairs, you'll still want a cordless or upright, which is where our [cheap Dyson alternative](/blog/cheap-dyson-alternative-uk-2026) guide comes in.
